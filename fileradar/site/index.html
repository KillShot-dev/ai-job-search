What it does

One job per view. Nothing hidden in menus.

Each view below does one thing well. Your files stay where they are — FileRadar organises how you see them, and cleanup sends things to the Trash, never straight to permanent deletion, so you can undo.

🗂️

Shelves by kind

Images, documents, videos, audio, archives and the rest, each on its own shelf — so a download has a visible home the moment it lands.

🔎

Natural-language search

Type like you talk — “invoices from last month” — and watch filter chips form from your words. Edit a chip to narrow the results.

📝

On-device OCR

Text inside images and scans becomes searchable, processed entirely on your Mac. Nothing is sent anywhere to make it work.

⬇️

Downloaded From grouping

See downloads grouped by where they came from, so you can retrace a file to its source instead of guessing.

👯

Duplicates

Find copies of the same file, compare them side by side, and choose what to keep. You confirm before anything moves to the Trash.

🧹

Clean Up

A calm review of large, old, or unneeded files, with a clear summary of what will go to the Trash — and an undo for mistakes.

⚡

Quick Search

Find a file from anywhere with a keyboard shortcut, without opening a window first.

▾

Menu bar

Recent files and Quick Search live in the menu bar, a click away, and you can drag files straight out of it.

🕰️

Timeline

Browse your files by when they arrived. Scrolling back through time is often faster than remembering a filename.

🗺️

Disk map

A visual map of what is using your disk space, so the biggest folders are obvious at a glance.

📚

Version stacks

Revisions of the same file stay stacked together, with the latest on top and earlier versions one click away.

✏️

Batch rename

Rename many files in one pass with a live preview of every new name before anything changes.

🏷️

Auto-tags

Tags suggested in plain words, with an explanation of why each one was suggested. You keep the final say.

📈

Storage forecast

A plain estimate of when your disk will fill up at your current pace, so a full disk does not surprise you.

🩺

File-health checks

Flags files that look incomplete, empty, or damaged, so problems surface before you need the file.

📦

Parcel export

Bundle a set of files into one tidy package to share or archive, with its contents listed plainly.

▦

Dual-pane mode

Optional, and off by default: turn on a second pane when you need it, and drag files between panes. The shelves view stays the default.

🗃️

Drop Stack

A temporary holding tray in the sidebar: drop files onto it while you gather them, then drop the whole stack where it belongs.

⏳

Transfer queue

In the app, every copy and move goes through one queue with progress, pause, and cancel — so bulk work never blocks the window.

🗂️

Tabs

Open several locations in tabs in one window. In the app, your tabs come back when you relaunch; in the demo, they last for the session.

💾

Saved workspaces

Named layouts that restore your sidebar, panes, and tabs in one click. A full-version feature — locked in the demo.

📏

Folder sizes, on demand

Sizes are worked out in the background and shown in place, with more ways to sort — never a blocking scan.

🔍

Modular inspector

Preview, Info, Tags, and Permissions as collapsible modules in one inspector. In the demo, Permissions are illustrative only.

🗜️

Archive browsing

Open an archive as if it were a folder, read-only, and extract only what you need, on demand. A full-version feature — locked in the demo.

⌘

Command palette

Press Cmd-K for every action, with full keyboard navigation — no need to hunt through menus.

⇄

Folder sync and compare

Preview what differs before anything happens; the only destructive step is moving previewed files to the Trash, after you confirm. A full-version feature — locked in the demo.

🌿

Git status badges

In the app, read-only Git status badges, opt-in and off by default — see a file's Git status at a glance without changing anything.

💬

Muse chat in FileRadar

Ask about your files in plain words and act on them from inside FileRadar, with your approval before anything moves. Chat is powered by Muse Spark through Meta's Model API and requires a Meta developer configuration. Your personal Muse app history and memory do not carry over into FileRadar, and FileRadar is not affiliated with or endorsed by Meta.

🔐

Extremely secure login

Sign in to Muse chat through a secure authorisation flow that exchanges a one-time code for access, and keep the resulting credentials in your Mac's Keychain — not in a plain file. Nothing about your sign-in is shared beyond what the login itself needs.

🎙️

Voice

Speak your request instead of typing it, and hear a spoken reply back, so you can ask about your files hands-free. Voice input and replies are part of the Muse chat experience in the app; the web demo does not include a live voice session.

About Muse chat: it is powered by Muse Spark through Meta's Model API and requires a Meta developer configuration. Your personal Muse app history and memory do not carry over into FileRadar. FileRadar is not affiliated with or endorsed by Meta.

Private by design

No account. Nothing uploaded. FileRadar works entirely on your Mac: indexing, search, and OCR all happen on-device, and your files never leave your computer to use it.

System requirements

macOS 14 or later. Works on Apple silicon and Intel Macs. FileRadar is a native Mac app with full keyboard navigation and VoiceOver support.

Questions, answered plainly

What is FileRadar?

A Finder alternative for Mac that organises your downloads and files into shelves by kind, helps you search in plain language, and helps you clean up duplicates and unneeded files.

Does FileRadar replace Finder?

No. Your files stay where they are, and Finder stays available. FileRadar gives you additional views — shelves, Timeline, disk map, Clean Up — for finding and tidying files.

Does FileRadar upload my files anywhere?

No. There is no account, and indexing, search, and OCR all run on your Mac. Nothing is uploaded.

What does FileRadar cost?

A one-time purchase of $8 for the first 60 days after launch, then $19 — for 1 Mac, in USD. There is no subscription. The launch date has not been announced yet, so the launch window has not started.

Can I try it before buying?

Yes — try the demo linked at the top of this page. A downloadable app is not available yet; it will be offered at launch.

What Macs does it run on?

macOS 14 or later, on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

What happens to files I clean up?

They go to the Trash, not to permanent deletion, so you can recover them. Bulk actions ask you to confirm first, and you can undo.
